# Stop fine-tuning, start fixing the mode decision

**Decision: do not switch to a CNN, and do not spend the next days on more backbone fine-tuning. Put the next effort into the discrete oversized-mode decision and the cached-feature stack (calibrated mode probability, expected-sMAPE decoding, distribution pooling, soft-gated kNN), and treat B4.0 as an ensemble member, not a successor to B3.4.** The reason is arithmetic from our own results. Each wrong-mode call costs about 2.0 sMAPE for that object, against roughly 0.2-0.4 for a right-mode call, so at ~10% oversized labels one extra percent of objects in the wrong mode costs about 2 points. That is the same size as the entire single-fold gap we are trying to resolve. B4.0 (fold-0 CV 40.28, board 40.46) did not beat B3.4 (40.82 CV, 38.37 board), and the biggest historical jumps came from adding diversity (DINOv3-H+, within-category kNN), not from adapting weights. The answer to "should we use CNN architecture since data is not that much?" is **no, not as a replacement; at most as a cheap frozen ensemble member**. The literature that favours CNNs in low-data regimes compares supervised ImageNet backbones, not self-supervised DINO-class ViTs, and we are already on frozen DINOv2-L/DINOv3-H+ features plus ridge and kNN, with a LoRA run that only matched that baseline. The priority order below carries expected gains that are **our researchers' guesses, not measurements**; every one must pass a paired, category-clustered bootstrap on out-of-fold predictions, since a 2-point single-fold gap is noise. Evidence quality is weak throughout: arXiv and GitHub fetches were blocked, so most venues, numbers and licences below are snippet-level, and where a claim has no source link it is flagged as inference.

| Priority | Action | Cost | Expected gain (guess, UNVERIFIED) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | OOF-calibrate P(oversized) (temperature/Platt or beta; category Beta-Binomial prior and kNN vote as inputs) | hours, CPU | largest single lever; 0.5-2 points |

## The mode decision is worth more than any backbone change

Noisy-label regression literature since 2023 is almost entirely about cleaning, which is the wrong goal here because the oversized labels are systematic and presumably present in the test set. ConFrag (NeurIPS 2024) and the 2024 Machine Learning sample-selection paper both filter suspect labels ([ConFrag](https://proceedings.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/file/e68c3a624d19154b28951e8690834607-Paper-Conference.pdf); [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10994-024-06637-5)); a 2026 benchmark summary reports small-loss selection failing on frozen foundation-model features ([Pith, primary unverified](https://pith.science/paper/2605.22591)). Applying any of them would delete exactly the objects that carry the score. No retrieved paper treats a unit-convention latent directly, so the design must come from decision theory plus mixture modelling, and our B3 pipeline (mode logistic plus within-mode regression) already sits in that family; the Bishop-style "most probable branch" reading of mixture density networks supports choosing a branch rather than averaging branches ([MDN overview, secondary](https://hunterheidenreich.com/notes/machine-learning/generative-models/mixture-density-networks/)).

The decision rule follows from the loss. The sMAPE term 2*tanh(|d|/2) saturates, so a factor-200 miss costs about 1.99 whichever way it goes, and hedging at the geometric midpoint costs about 1.74 against either truth, whereas a hard choice at p=0.5 costs at most about 1 in expectation. This is our own derivation from the metric, not a published result, but it is simple enough to trust: hedging never wins, the threshold sits near 0.5 shifted slightly towards the mode with smaller within-mode error, and the real lever is the quality of the probability. Gneiting's framework says the optimal point forecast is the Bayes rule for the scoring function ([Gneiting, arXiv 0912.0902](https://arxiv.org/pdf/0912.0902)), and Kolassa (Forecasting, 2026) states that for sMAPE it must be found by numerically minimising expected sMAPE under the predictive distribution, because sMAPE elicits no standard functional ([MDPI](https://www.mdpi.com/2571-9394/8/2/24)). The practical decoder is therefore one-dimensional: build a grid over ln(volume) from the calibrated mixture and take the argmin of the expected loss. TabPFN's regression API exposes a comparable custom-loss decoding on a bar distribution ([Prior Labs docs](https://docs.priorlabs.ai/capabilities/regression)), the closest precedent found. No paper compares this against a hard switch, and our own derivation says the two differ mainly in threshold and in picking a windowed mode inside the chosen component, so the earlier B3.6 finding that decoding tweaks gave no gain is consistent with this, and priority 2 should be expected to be smaller than priority 1.

Calibration is where the evidence is firmest. A cost-sensitive threshold is only valid on calibrated probabilities ([calibration survey, Mach. Learn. 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10994-023-06336-7)); Platt scaling works with little data but assumes a sigmoid distortion, isotonic needs more data, and beta calibration beat logistic calibration on 41 datasets in the 2017 study ([Kull et al., AISTATS 2017](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v54/kull17a/kull17a.pdf)). With 2,042 objects and ~200 oversized, prefer Platt or beta on out-of-fold logits, and use isotonic only if the calibration set reaches several hundred positives. With 164 categories and a median of 8 objects (48 categories with three or fewer), the category oversize rate needs Beta-Binomial shrinkage towards a coarse food-group prior rather than raw frequency ([empirical Bayes notes](https://metricgate.com/docs/hierarchical-beta-binomial-baseball/); [Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Empirical_Bayes_method)). Feed shrunk rate, kNN vote and logistic score into one stacked logit and score it by Brier and log loss as well as by decoded sMAPE. The within-mode regressors should be trained per mode, so the base head never sees oversized labels; that removes the contamination without needing robust losses.

## Retrieval gains come from gating and correction, and leakage needs nested folds

Published learned-metric methods are poor fits for our regime. Rank-N-Contrast (NeurIPS 2023), ConR (ICLR 2024) and RankSim (ICML 2022) are end-to-end losses evaluated on large age-like benchmarks ([RnC](https://github.com/kaiwenzha/Rank-N-Contrast); [ConR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.06651); [RankSim](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.15236)), and we found no evidence for them on frozen ViT features at 2k images. A rank-based loss would also be dominated by the 200-1000x outliers unless the target is log-transformed and the mode handled separately (inference). The deep imbalanced regression family generally (LDS/FDS, Balanced MSE, VIR) targets a smooth density tail, whereas our imbalance is categorical and mode-based ([Balanced MSE, CVPR 2022](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/CVPR2022/papers/Ren_Balanced_MSE_for_Imbalanced_Visual_Regression_CVPR_2022_paper.pdf)); skip it. The neighbour-aware heads are the relevant family: TabR (ICLR 2024) treats retrieval as attention over the m nearest candidates ([ICLR](https://proceedings.iclr.cc/paper_files/paper/2024/hash/4ef594af0d9a519db8fb292452c461fa-Abstract-Conference.html)), and a 2025 age-estimation preprint retrieves look-alike references and predicts differences against them ([arXiv 2502.04852](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.04852), venue unconfirmed). That second design is the closest analogue to our k=2 twin lookup, and it motivates the "retrieve-then-correct" experiment: predict ln(y_query/y_neighbour) by ridge on the feature difference and average over the top neighbours, taking care that neighbours from the other mode are excluded.

Our B3.4 jump (44.35 to 40.19 on fresh seeds) says near twins carry most of the within-category signal, and no source tests k=1-2 or distance gating, so the recommendation is ours: softmax-over-cosine weights within category, in ln space, with a fallback to ridge when the top-1 similarity falls below a threshold, both tuned by nested CV. This subsumes the choice of k. Expect it to help mainly by not trusting weak neighbours.

Leakage with fine-tuned embeddings is the one place where the notes found no verifiable literature, so what follows is standard practice reasoned from first principles, not a cited finding. If the encoder has seen an object's label, its embedding sits unusually close to its own training neighbours and the kNN or ridge head fitted on those embeddings looks better than it will on test. The safe protocol is nested out-of-fold: for each fold, fine-tune on the other folds, embed the held-out objects, retrieve only from that fold's training bank, and score only held-out rows. The final submission uses the all-train encoder and all-train bank, which matches the test condition because test twins are not in train. Where a within-train kNN is needed for stacking, drop self from the candidate set (TabR is reported to do this, unverified). Do not group folds by twin clusters if roughly a third of test objects genuinely have a twin in train, since that would measure a harder problem than the board; keep the natural rate. Related leakage evidence exists only in a different field, where near-duplicates inflated retrieval benchmarks and changed system rankings ([arXiv 2206.14759](https://arxiv.org/html/2206.14759v2)). This is also why the B4.0 blend test is designed correctly: only its five out-of-fold predictions may be used to fit blend weights.

## Heads, ensembles and the fine-tuning recipe

Distributional heads have real support but not for our regime. HL-Gauss won on Atari, scaling and robotics tasks and was more robust to noisy targets ([Farebrother et al., ICML 2024](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.5555/3692070.3692593)); the JMLR study of the histogram loss concludes the benefit is better optimisation rather than extra distributional information, with a rule of thumb of about 100 bins and sigma of twice the bin width, on fairly large datasets ([JMLR 27](http://jmlr.org/papers/v27/24-0260.html)); decoding-based regression found distributional heads often beat pointwise ones especially with little data, but the histogram head needs more data as bins increase ([TMLR 2025](https://mlanthology.org/tmlr/2025/song2025tmlr-decodingbased/)). No paper tests these on a small, label-contaminated set. Our judgement is that a histogram head on the within-mode residual, with 32-64 bins on a restricted range rather than 100, may buy modest robustness and gives the decoder in priority 2 a native input, but it is not a first-order lever. Free-K mixture density networks are reported unstable ([arXiv 2603.22328](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.22328v1), preprint); a two-component mixture with a calibrated gate is safer at n=2,042. Evidential heads are untested in the notes, and ordinal CORN evidence is on age labels ([Springer 2023](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10044-023-01181-9)).

For ensembling, pool distributions, not points. Averaging in ln space destroys the bimodality the decoder needs. Linear pooling keeps both modes and is overdispersive, while Vincentization keeps sharpness and suits unimodal members ([Schulz and Lerch, Mach. Learn. 2025](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10994-025-06946-3)); linear pools tend to be underconfident and benefit from a post-pool temperature ([Rahaman and Thiery, NeurIPS 2021](https://papers.neurips.cc/paper_files/paper/2021/file/a70dc40477bc2adceef4d2c90f47eb82-Paper.pdf)). With five-ish parameters (member weights, one temperature, one threshold) fitted on grouped OOF predictions, overfitting at n=2,042 is manageable; a flexible stacker is not. Conformal methods add coverage guarantees but no sMAPE benefit, so skip them. TabPFN regression is a possible extra member, but its weight licence for competition use is unverified and should be checked first ([Nature 2025](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41586-024-08328-6)).

On ViT fine-tuning, the notes support a modest next step rather than a new programme. B4.0 already sits in the evidence-backed region of all-layer attention LoRA at low rank; rank matters mainly under large domain gaps ([SR-LoRA, arXiv 2507.00327](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.00327), venue unverified) and adapters confined to some depths underperformed all-layer adapters in CLIP few-shot work ([arXiv 2505.15130](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15130v3)). The closest published analogue is a DINOv3 LoRA regression on 357 pasture images where full fine-tuning overfit, the frozen backbone could not adapt, and depth-growing rank was the remedy ([Sensors 2026](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC13517550/), single dataset). LP-FT showed that a random head distorts pretrained features early ([Kumar et al., ICLR 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2202.10054), classification only), and our setup trains a fresh head at 1e-3 alongside LoRA, which is the pattern it warns about; initialising the head from the frozen-feature ridge, or two head-only epochs, is cheap. EMA is the best-supported stabiliser ([arXiv 2212.06138](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2212.06138)), with a lower momentum (about 0.99-0.999) suiting our few hundred steps per epoch (inference). Because held-out score was still improving at epoch 10, a longer 20-epoch schedule is the likeliest single fix. Layer-wise decay and drop path follow MAE practice ([MAE FINETUNE.md](https://github.com/facebookresearch/mae/blob/main/FINETUNE.md)). On the 6 GB card, token count is the cost lever: 512 px gives 1,024 patches, and 384-448 px would cut time substantially (estimated, untested), possibly enabling more epochs or folds; test 384 against 512 once. Skip DoRA, LoRA+, rsLoRA, VeRA and IA3 (no ViT-L evidence found), and C-Mixup: mixing volume labels across rendered images is geometrically wrong. Keep the auxiliary mesh weight at 0.1; the food multi-task literature is small-scale ([arXiv 2004.13188](https://arxiv.org/html/2004.13188)) and the effect would be hidden by fold noise. The most reliable gain from fine-tuning is probably blending: fold or seed averaging plus flip TTA, and stacking with the frozen-feature stack, which is exactly what the in-progress 5-fold B4.0 test measures. Frozen DINOv2 with kNN is repeatedly competitive at low data ([Pith summary, primary unverified](https://pith.science/paper/2605.14145)), and DINOv2 authors describe fine-tuning as a small, last-step gain ([DINOv2 paper](https://arxiv.org/html/2304.07193v2)), consistent with B4.0 landing level with kNN.

## CNNs are a diversity play, and scale priors from geometry models are a long shot

The CNN evidence is real but does not match our competitor. A 2024 low-data study found Swin-style attention backbones doing worse than ConvNeXt, EfficientNet and RegNet in fine-tuning ([arXiv 2406.05612](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.05612v1), venue unverified), and one 10k-image benchmark showed ConvNeXt only about 1-2 points above ViT variants ([arXiv 2509.23100](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.23100v1)). At scale, architecture matters little: NFNets matched ViTs at comparable compute ([Smith et al., arXiv 2310.16764](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.16764)). None of these compares against frozen self-supervised DINO features with kNN, which is our incumbent, and our LoRA run matching that baseline suggests the bottleneck is label and scale ambiguity, not inductive bias. For a concrete conv option, DINOv3 ships distilled ConvNeXt students (Tiny 29M to Large 198M) that the paper says beat supervised ImageNet-22K counterparts ([DINOv3 repo](https://github.com/facebookresearch/dinov3); [arXiv 2508.10104](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.10104v1)); inference-only extraction at 512 px with multi-stage pooled features takes minutes on 2.2k images, uses the existing ridge/kNN pipeline, and carries the same custom DINOv3 licence as the ViT-H+ we already use (full terms unread). Adopt it only if its OOF residual correlation with the DINO stack is low, since ensemble gains track diversity ([arXiv 2504.09076](https://arxiv.org/html/2504.09076)). Handcrafted silhouette descriptors from the grey backdrop (area fraction, aspect, solidity, elongation) are an equally cheap block (inference). Skip ConvNeXt-V2 (non-commercial weights, no advantage evidence, [repo](https://github.com/facebookresearch/ConvNeXt-V2)), ConvNeXt-L fine-tuning (memory), 1024 px passes (auto-framing normalises object extent, so extra pixels add texture, not scale), and from-scratch CNNs. MoGe-2 is itself a DINOv2 ViT-L with conv necks, and its ablation found an MLP on the CLS token predicts metric scale better than a conv head ([NeurIPS 2025](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.02546v1)), so even the geometry literature does not endorse conv heads for a global scalar.

Scale-from-appearance priors have the thinnest support of all six topics. LLMs do poorly at zero-shot size comparison ([POSQA, EMNLP Findings 2023](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.13394)), VLMs gain metric knowledge only after spatial training ([SpatialVLM](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2401.12168)), and the reference-object methods need a reference our renders lack. Metric-depth models convert output to metres using focal length in pixels, which auto-framing breaks ([DA3METRIC-LARGE](https://huggingface.co/depth-anything/DA3METRIC-LARGE)). Single-image-to-3D generators do not fit a 6 GB card: TRELLIS.2 officially needs 24 GB ([repo](https://github.com/microsoft/trellis.2)), and SAM 3D Objects reportedly needs V100-class memory ([issue 6](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam-3d-objects/issues/6)); no volume or fill-ratio accuracy was published for any of them. No 2024-2026 method recovering absolute size from a scale-free auto-framed image was found, so this is an unanswered question, not a negative result. Two checks are cheap enough to run opportunistically: a one-off LLM query of per-category typical sizes, kept only if it correlates with train category medians, and MoGe-2 encoder features tested by residual R-squared gain over DINO. Both are speculative, with expected gains under a few points of within-category R-squared by our own guess.

## Validation discipline decides which of these survive

The board runs 1.5-2.5 points below CV and has a standard error near 2.4 points, so leaderboard moves under about 5 points are noise unless supported by CV. The acceptance gate should be a paired bootstrap on per-object sMAPE differences ([arXiv 2511.19794](https://arxiv.org/html/2511.19794v1), which warns that unpaired tests often falsely signal 0.6-2.0 point gains), resampled by category rather than object because objects within a category are correlated (our inference), repeated over different fold partitions ([PMC 6191021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6191021/)), and reported together with the number of flipped mode decisions, since the score is driven by those flips. Evaluate with object-level folds when test categories overlap train, and with leave-category-out folds only for the shrinkage prior of rare categories.

## Conclusion

The notes converge on a reframing: with ~10% systematic outliers and a saturating metric, this is a classification-plus-regression problem whose score is governed by how many objects land in the wrong mode, so the next gains are more likely in probability calibration, decoding and distribution pooling than in new backbones, CNN or ViT. That is also the cheapest work available, since it runs on cached features and a CPU.

What remains genuinely unknown is large. No source tests any of these methods on a contaminated, 2k-image, scale-free regression task; every expected gain above is a guess; leakage handling for fine-tuned retrieval rests on practice, not literature; and several licences (TabPFN weights, DINOv3 full terms, MoGe-2 weights) were never read. The measured B4.0 five-fold blend, together with the calibration and decoding experiments, will answer more than further reading will.
