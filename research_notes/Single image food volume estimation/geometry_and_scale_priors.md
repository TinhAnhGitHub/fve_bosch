# Monocular geometry, single-image 3D reconstruction and learned size priors for absolute volume from one auto-framed render

Scope note. Research date 2026-11-04. "Verified" means the claim was read this session from the primary repo, licence file, model card or arXiv page linked beside it. "Prior knowledge, not re-verified" means it comes from the researcher's background knowledge (cutoff mid-2026) and the linked repo was not fetched this session. Those claims must be checked before the report relies on them. Competition context: 1024x1024 Blender render of one food item on a grey backdrop, auto-framed so the object fills about 20% of the frame, no intrinsics, no reference object. Target is volume_cm3, scored with sMAPE. Volume = size^3 x fill factor (train median fill 0.45 +/- 0.13).

## Literature map: models, licences, scale handling and cost

### Takeaway
Every metric depth model infers absolute scale from scene context plus an estimated or given focal length. An auto-framed object on a plain backdrop removes that context, so their "metric" output collapses to a learned appearance prior. Every single-image-to-3D generator outputs a shape normalised to a unit cube, which suits a scale-free shape and fill-factor feature. The licence screen for a Bosch (EU) internal competition rules out Hunyuan3D 2.x (it excludes EU territory), UniDepth, Cube R-CNN, the non-commercial Depth Anything V2/V3 checkpoints, the original VGGT checkpoint, and SF3D/SPAR3D (Stability's revenue threshold). The clean options are MoGe/MoGe-2, Depth Anything V2-Small, DA3 Small/Base/Metric-Large/Mono-Large, Marigold v1-0, Depth Pro (custom Apple licence), TRELLIS/TRELLIS.2 and SAM 3D Objects (SAM License, royalty-free).

- MetaFood CVPR 2024 challenge: volume-accurate 3D food reconstruction with a **visible checkerboard as the size reference**. 20 items in easy (200 images), medium (30) and hard (1 image) tiers; the last five items used single-view methods. 16 teams; scored with MAPE and Chamfer distance. ## Which models give useful features for an auto-framed, context-free single-object render, and what do papers say about OOD / context-free failure?

### Takeaway
Under auto-framing with a fixed (unknown) render camera, image geometry is identical whatever the object's true size. Perspective, silhouette and depth ratios are scale-invariant, so no geometry model can recover absolute size from geometry. Any "metric" output reflects a learned appearance and category prior. Documented failure modes such as WildMoGe's "scale-collapse" show metric heads lean on training-distribution context. Relative and normal outputs (shape, thickness, depth range / extent ratio) remain useful scale-free features.

### Cited Findings
- Metric depth models get scale from intrinsics plus context. Depth Pro and UniDepth estimate focal length / intrinsics from the image ([Depth Pro](https://github.com/apple/ml-depth-pro), [UniDepth](https://github.com/lpiccinelli-eth/UniDepth)). DA3METRIC-LARGE needs focal length in pixels to convert its output to meters ([DA3](https://github.com/ByteDance-Seed/Depth-Anything-3)). MoGe-2 accepts an optional GT FOV to "enhance model accuracy further" ([MoGe](https://github.com/microsoft/MoGe)).
- "Size-to-depth": "with fixed focal length, real-world size is proportional to depth" — [arXiv 1801.04461](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1801.04461). With auto-framing, the camera distance scales with object size, so the image is the same up to texture detail.
- Metric-scale models show systematic "scale-collapse": far-field structures are "metrically shrunk and pulled toward the camera". The authors attribute it to training data dominated by vehicle LiDAR, short-range indoor scans and synthetic data. MoGe-2 estimates the Arc de Triomphe at 18.8 m vs 44.8 m real. Fine-tuning on in-the-wild data (WildMoGe) gives 43.4 m. Foreground subjects stay consistent (1.4 m). On indoor and street scenes WildMoGe and MoGe-2 give similar scales — [arXiv 2606.02379](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.02379), [project page](https://metricscenes.github.io/). This shows the metric output depends on the training distribution rather than geometric evidence.
- Single View Metrology in the Wild needs **co-occurring reference categories** (humans, cars) for absolute scale — [arXiv 2007.09529](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2007.09529). The competition images lack such references.
- Appearance cues are "only weakly correlated with absolute object size and are often entangled with lighting conditions, viewpoint, and surface reflectance" — [arXiv 2601.20303](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.20303).
- Even in the MetaFood food-volume challenge, the organisers supplied a checkerboard for scale — [arXiv 2407.09285](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.09285).

### Inferences
- Expected behaviour of MoGe-2 / Depth Pro / DA3-Nested on these renders: the predicted object extent will be roughly a function of recognised category ("apple-sized", "watermelon-sized") plus texture granularity. That is a weak learned prior, probably worse than a supervised head trained on the 2,042 labelled objects. The metric output is cheap to test as one more feature (MoGe-2 ViT-L about 60 ms/image; ViT-S 35M params), but do not expect it to beat a fine-tuned classifier/regressor on 164 categories.
- Useful scale-free outputs: normalised depth range (thickness / visible extent), normal maps, and the visible-surface point map. Together with the silhouette these approximate the shape-fill factor. FOV estimates may be near-constant across renders (one Blender camera). If so, they confirm the render camera but add no scale signal.
- Possible scale cues in Blender renders, not from the literature and to be tested: texture-map resolution and detail per pixel, shading and specular scale, depth-of-field if enabled, and the shadow/contact region on the backdrop. These are dataset artefacts that a fine-tuned backbone could exploit.
- Error propagation: volume scales with size^3, so a 10% linear-size error gives about 33% volume error (1.1^3 = 1.33). The scale head dominates sMAPE. A perfect fill factor removes only the +/-0.13 (about 29% relative) fill variance.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that evaluates metric depth models specifically on object-centric renders with uniform backgrounds, or on auto-framed inputs. The context-free failure claim is inferred from the mechanism and from the scale-collapse evidence, not measured.
- No quantitative OOD numbers for MoGe-2 / Depth Pro / UniDepth on Objaverse-style or turntable renders were found this session.

## Is it practical to reconstruct a mesh and compute a normalised shape volume (volume / extent^3) as a feature, plus a separate learned scale? Cost per image?

### Takeaway
Yes, technically. TRELLIS.2 (MIT) runs at about 3-17 s per image on an H100 depending on resolution and needs a GPU with at least 24 GB. SAM 3D Objects (royalty-free SAM License) also gives a layout scale, but that scale comes from MoGe point maps. For about 2,000 training plus test images that is roughly 2-10 GPU-hours, which is affordable. The open question is fidelity of the unseen back side and of watertightness for volume, since generators hallucinate occluded geometry. Hunyuan3D 2.x is cheaper on VRAM (10 GB for shape) but cannot be used in the EU.

### Cited Findings
- TRELLIS.2-4B: single image -> PBR 3D asset. H100 timings ~17 s at 1024^3 and ~60 s at 1536^3; ~3 s at 512^3 is reported on a mirror only. Needs >=24 GB, tested on A100/H100, Linux. MIT licence — [HF model card](https://huggingface.co/microsoft/TRELLIS.2-4B/blob/main/README.md).
- Hunyuan3D 2.1: 10 GB VRAM for shape, 29 GB for shape plus texture — [GitHub](https://github.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-2.1). Licence territory excludes the EU, UK and South Korea — [LICENSE](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/Tencent-Hunyuan/Hunyuan3D-2.1/main/LICENSE).
- SAM 3D Objects: image + mask -> shape, texture and layout (R, t, s) in camera coordinates ([GitHub](https://github.com/facebookresearch/sam-3d-objects); [papernotes summary](https://en.papernotes.org/CVPR2026/3d_vision/sam_3d_3dfy_anything_in_images/)). MoGe point maps are the geometric input. A 2026 follow-up replaces MoGe with DA3 metric point maps ([arXiv 2603.11633](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.11633)). Reported layout ADD-S@0.1 of 77% (secondary source).
- MetaFood 2024 had a single-view (1-image) tier for food 3D reconstruction scored with volume MAPE and Chamfer, which shows single-view food reconstruction for volume has been attempted. Per-team single-view numbers were not retrieved — [arXiv 2407.09285](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.09285).

### Inferences
- Recommended feature: reconstruct with TRELLIS.2 (or TRELLIS v1 / SAM 3D), make the mesh watertight (voxelise if needed), then compute V_norm = mesh_volume / (bbox extent product) and convex-hull ratio, plus aspect ratios. These estimate the "fill factor" target. They can be supervised directly on train, because training meshes give the true fill. A simpler baseline is to regress fill directly from image features: the shape signal is mostly in the silhouette and shading, and a 2,042-sample regressor may match the noisy reconstruction.
- Check the generator's normalisation convention (unit cube, longest side = 1). Volume / extent^3 using the longest extent is invariant to it. Volume / (Lx * Ly * Lz) needs an axis convention consistent with how train "bbox" is defined.
- Orientation matters: the competition bbox is probably axis-aligned in the mesh frame, while generator output is canonicalised differently. Use orientation-invariant descriptors (volume / OBB volume, volume / convex-hull volume, sphericity) to avoid mismatch.
- Separate learned scale: fine-tune a backbone (DINOv2/v3, CLIP, MoGe encoder) on log(max extent) or log(volume) with category as an auxiliary target. The scale prior is mostly the category-typical size (see next question).
- Cost: about 2,000-4,000 images x 3-17 s on an H100 is about 2-19 GPU-hours, which is practical offline. Inference-time cost per test image would be 3-60 s, so check whether the competition has a runtime limit.

### Gaps
- No published accuracy of TRELLIS / SAM 3D / Hunyuan3D volume relative to ground truth on food meshes was found. Back-side hallucination error on volume is unquantified.
- SAM 3D Objects latency and VRAM were not documented on the README.

## What evidence exists that VLMs or vision backbones encode absolute object size? Accuracy numbers?

### Takeaway
VLMs encode coarse category-typical sizes, and benchmark designers explicitly worry that models "lean on typical-size priors". Measured metric accuracy is mediocre: GPT-4o scores about 44-69 MRA on VSI-Bench object size (column mapping uncertain), and on SIRI-Bench the best model keeps only about 31% of predictions within 40% error. For a 164-category food set, a VLM or zero-shot backbone gives a category-level size prior at best. A supervised head on the 2,042 training objects should beat it.

### Cited Findings
- VSI-Bench (Thinking in Space, CVPR 2025): numerical answers including object size are scored with Mean Relative Accuracy averaged over thresholds 0.5-0.95 ([arXiv 2603.23404](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2603.23404)). GPT-4o object-size scores read as about 68.6 (64 frames) and 43.8 (16 frames); Gemini-1.5 Flash about 56.5. Column alignment was inferred from the table layout and is unconfirmed — [Thinking in Space site](https://vision-x-nyu.github.io/thinking-in-space.github.io). Inputs are video of indoor scenes, not single context-free images.
- A 2025 holistic evaluation reports GPT-5 exceeding human performance on object size under MRA. Exact numbers not extracted — [arXiv 2508.13142](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.13142).
- ReVSI rebuilds the VSI-Bench object-size evaluation with per-category size ranges in cm, so that models cannot rely on typical-size priors instead of the image — [arXiv 2604.24300](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.24300).
- SIRI-Bench: the best VLM keeps only about 31% of predictions within 40% error. The authors partly attribute this to difficulty estimating object dimensions — [arXiv 2506.14512](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2506.14512).
- Metric-Bench (ECCV 2026): infer absolute metric sizes from sparse reference objects without intrinsics on ScanNetV2; current VLMs are better at metric depth than at width/height — [arXiv 2609.25841](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.25841v1).
- VLM-3R: object-size scores are roughly flat (about 70) when adding reconstruction-derived inputs, while absolute-distance gains are larger — [arXiv 2505.20279](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.20279).
- Tri-Bench: about 69% average accuracy against 3D ground truth on simple spatial tasks; models tend to reason in the 2D image plane — [arXiv 2512.08860](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.08860v1).
- Text-only LLM size knowledge: on size-comparison cloze probes, large LMs use prepositions ("in", "into") to infer size relations but fall back on prior physical commonsense rather than verbs ([Kondo et al., ACL 2023](https://aclanthology.org/2023.acl-short.53.pdf)). POSQA has about 12k pairwise object-size questions ([arXiv 2310.13394](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.13394v1)). Its results were not retrieved.

### Inferences
- In this setting a "typical-size prior" is a feature, not a bug: with no image scale cue, the category-conditional size distribution is the main signal. A cheap, licence-clean use of LLMs/VLMs is offline: ask for typical dimensions or volume per category name for the 164 categories, as a prior feature or for cold-start on rare categories. Verify against train medians. Per-image VLM querying adds latency and probably little within-category signal.
- Within-category size variation (for example small vs large apple) needs texture or detail cues that only a fine-tuned backbone could pick up. No paper found quantifies this for context-free renders.
- SpatialVLM / SpatialRGPT metric labels come from metric depth pipelines, so they inherit the same context dependence and give no extra scale evidence for isolated objects (inference from their data-generation design; not re-verified this session).

### Gaps
- No benchmark found tests VLM absolute-size estimation on isolated objects without context (closest: ReVSI's category-range design). No linear-probe study of DINOv2/CLIP features for absolute object size was found this session.
- Gemini 2.x/3 and GPT-5 object-size numbers on VSI-Bench were not extracted.
- Whether VLM licences (for example Qwen-VL, Llama-vision) permit Bosch internal use was outside the checks done here.
